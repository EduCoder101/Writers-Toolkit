Stage 3 · A poetry study

The Book
of Feelings

“Hope” is the thing with feathers –
That perches in the soul –
And sings the tune without the words –
And never stops – at all –

Emily Dickinson

Dickinson never tells us what hope means. She hands us a small bird instead, and lets the bird do the telling. Inside this book are pictures, words and rhymes for twenty feelings of your own. Borrow whatever helps. Then close the lid, pick up your pen, and write.

What we are learning

We are learning to write a poem that uses a symbol as a metaphor for an abstract noun, holding a repeated pattern and rhyme across several stanzas.

  • I can choose an abstract noun and explain what it means to me.
  • I can pick a symbol and use it as a metaphor to stand for my abstract noun.
  • I can use repetition by starting each stanza with the same “[Idea] is…” pattern.
  • I can use rhyme by ending lines with words that share a sound.
  • Extension: I can extend one metaphor through a whole stanza so every line adds to the same picture.

A way in

Now it is yours. Close the lid, and write your own opening in your book. Say your feeling is the thing, not like the thing: that is what makes it a metaphor.

The rhyming shelf

Every family in the book.

Find the sound your line ends on, and see who its relatives are. If your word is not here, listen for its last sound and hunt the nearest family. A near rhyme is allowed.
