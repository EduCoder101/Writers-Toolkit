The Book
of Feelings

“Hope” is the thing with feathers –
That perches in the soul –
And sings the tune without the words –
And never stops – at all –

Emily Dickinson

Dickinson never tells us what hope means. She hands us a small bird instead, and lets the bird do the telling. Inside this book are pictures, words and rhymes for twenty feelings of your own. Borrow whatever helps. Then close the lid, pick up your pen, and write.

What we are learning

We are learning to write a poem that uses a symbol as a metaphor for an abstract noun, holding a repeated pattern and rhyme across several stanzas.

  • I can choose an abstract noun and explain what it means to me.
  • I can pick a symbol and use it as a metaphor to stand for my abstract noun.
  • I can use repetition by starting each stanza with the same “[Idea] is…” pattern.
  • I can use rhyme by ending lines with words that share a sound.
  • Extension: I can extend one metaphor through a whole stanza so every line adds to the same picture.

Before you write

Three sample poems.

This is the standard to aim for. These sound like Year 5 poets, not famous grown-ups, because that is exactly what your poem should sound like: yours. Read all three, then steal the moves, not the words.

Courage is a Lion

Courage is a lion with a golden mane,
It stands up tall in the pouring rain.
Courage is a roar that shakes the trees,
It keeps on going with shaking knees.

Courage is a lion who guards the gate,
It doesn’t run and it doesn’t wait.
Courage is the first paw on the track,
Even when it wants to turn back.

Courage is a lion inside of me,
Quiet and small where no one can see.
It wakes up right when I need it most,
And roars at the things that scare me most.

Notice: the lion is in every stanza, and the poet lets it feel scared too. Brave and shaking can happen at the same time.

Joy is a Kite

Joy is a kite on a windy day,
It tugs at my hand and wants to play.
Joy is the string unwinding fast,
Laughing at clouds as they go past.

Joy is a kite that loops and dives,
It wakes up the sky and makes it alive.
Joy is the colour against the blue,
A little red dot the sun shines through.

Joy is a kite I can’t quite hold,
Brighter than treasure, better than gold.
And when it finally comes back down,
It pulls the sky all over town.

Notice: the kite does something different in every stanza: tugging, looping, coming home. That is an extended metaphor.

Loneliness is an Empty Room

Loneliness is an empty room,
Where dust floats round in the afternoon.
The chairs still sit where they always sat,
But nobody’s there to notice that.

Loneliness is a quiet clock,
Ticking away with no one to talk.
It sounds much louder than it should,
I’d turn it off if I only could.

But loneliness is a door as well,
Waiting for footsteps, waiting for the bell.
And when my friend knocks twice for me,
The room is as full as it can be.

Notice: clock and talk are a near rhyme, and that is allowed. And see how the poem turns at the end, from empty to full.

The rhyming shelf

Every family in the book.

Find the sound your line ends on, and see who its relatives are. If your word is not here, listen for its last sound and hunt the nearest family. A near rhyme is allowed.
