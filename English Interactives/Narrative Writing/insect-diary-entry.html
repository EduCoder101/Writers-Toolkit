📓 Diary Entry: Eating Insects

Write a diary entry describing how it felt to eat insects.

⏱ 20:00

📋 Your Task

Write a diary entry as one of the children describing how it felt eating the insects. Your paragraph must include:

  • Sensory details (taste, texture, smell)
  • Thoughts and emotions (fear, disgust, bravery)
  • Reasons for eating the insects
  • Reflection (Would you do it again?)

You must also include at least one adverbial phrase and one appositive.

🧠 Grammar Reminders

Adverbial Phrase

A group of words that tells the reader when, where, how or why something happened.

I picked up the insect with trembling fingers. HOW
Before I could change my mind, I put it in my mouth. WHEN
Appositive

A noun phrase that renames or describes another noun right next to it. Often sits between commas.

The cricket, a brown, crunchy creature no bigger than my thumb, sat on the plate.
Lina, the bravest in our group, went first.

✍️ Model Diary Entry

Read this example. Notice how the grammar features are used and how the writer includes sensory details, emotions and reflection.

Dear Diary,

Today I did something I never thought I would do. I ate a cricket. The plate sat in front of me, piled high with roasted insects, and my stomach twisted into a knot. The cricket, a small, golden-brown creature with spindly legs, stared up at me as if daring me to try. With a deep breath and shaking hands, I picked one up and placed it on my tongue. The shell cracked like a tiny chip, and a nutty, smoky flavour spread through my mouth. It was not what I expected. Part of me wanted to spit it out, but another part felt strangely proud. We had to eat them because there was nothing else, and wasting food was not an option. Despite my fear, I swallowed. Would I do it again? Honestly, I think I would. Not because I enjoyed it, but because I proved to myself that I could.
Adverbial phrase
Appositive

🔍 What to Notice

  • The appositive tells us what the cricket looked like, adding a sensory detail right where it matters.
  • The adverbial phrases tell us how the writer picked up the insect and describe their emotional state (despite my fear).
  • The paragraph moves through a clear sequence: the moment before, the moment of eating, the sensory experience, the reason, and the reflection.
⏱ 3 minutes

Jot down a few notes before you write. You do not need full sentences here.

🔤 Sentence Starters You Could Use

OpeningDear Diary, today I did something that made my stomach churn.
Describing the insect (appositive opportunity)The [insect], a __________, sat on the plate in front of me.
The moment of eating (adverbial opportunity)With __________, I lifted it to my mouth and...
Sensory detailThe taste was... / It crunched like... / A strange flavour of...
EmotionPart of me wanted to... but another part felt...
ReasonI had no choice because... / The reason I ate it was...
ReflectionIf someone asked me to do it again, I would... because...

These are suggestions to get you started. You do not have to use them.

📊 Rubric

This is how your diary entry will be assessed. Read through the criteria before you start writing in your book.

Criteria Exceptional (4) Proficient (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1)
Sensory Details Vivid, specific details across multiple senses (taste, texture, smell) that make the reader feel as though they are there. Clear sensory details covering at least two senses. The reader can picture the experience. Some sensory detail is present, but it is vague or limited to one sense only. Little or no sensory detail. The writing tells the reader what happened but does not describe how it felt.
Thoughts & Emotions Emotions are layered and develop across the entry (e.g. fear turns to pride). Inner thoughts feel genuine and specific. Emotions are clearly stated and connected to the experience. The reader understands how the character felt. Some emotion is mentioned, but it is general (e.g. "I felt scared") without development or detail. Emotions are missing or only named in passing with no connection to the experience.
Reason A clear, convincing reason is given that fits naturally within the diary entry and connects to the character's situation. A reason is given and makes sense in context. A reason is mentioned but is vague or feels disconnected from the rest of the entry. No reason is given for eating the insects.
Reflection Thoughtful reflection that goes beyond yes/no. The writer explains their thinking and shows personal growth or self-awareness. A clear reflection is included with a reason (e.g. "I would because..."). A brief reflection is present but lacks reasoning or depth. No reflection is included.
Adverbial Phrase Two or more adverbial phrases are used effectively, adding detail about when, where, how or why. They are correctly placed and enhance the writing. At least one adverbial phrase is used correctly and adds clear detail to a sentence. An adverbial phrase is attempted but may be a single adverb rather than a phrase, or is awkwardly placed. No adverbial phrase is included, or the attempt does not meet the definition.
Appositive Two or more appositives are used effectively to add relevant detail about a noun. They are correctly punctuated with commas. At least one appositive is used correctly, adding useful detail and punctuated with commas. An appositive is attempted but may be missing commas, or the phrase does not clearly rename or describe a noun. No appositive is included, or the attempt does not meet the definition.
Diary Voice & Structure Consistent first-person voice throughout. The entry reads naturally as a diary, with a clear beginning, middle and end. Writing flows smoothly between ideas. Written in first person with a diary-like tone. The entry has a logical structure. Mostly first person, but the diary voice is inconsistent or the structure is unclear. Not written as a diary entry, or switches frequently out of first person.
🎯
Aim for the Proficient column as your target. If you want to push yourself, look at what it takes to reach Exceptional.

✅ Self-Check

Read back through your diary entry in your book and tick off each item. If something is missing, go back and add it.

  • I have written in first person (I, me, my) as a diary entry.
  • I have included at least one adverbial phrase (a group of words telling when, where, how or why).
  • I have included at least one appositive (a noun phrase that renames or describes another noun, set off by commas).
  • I have included sensory details (taste, texture, smell).
  • I have described my thoughts and emotions.
  • I have explained why I ate the insects.
  • I have included a reflection (Would I do it again?).
  • I have re-read my work and checked my spelling and punctuation.
