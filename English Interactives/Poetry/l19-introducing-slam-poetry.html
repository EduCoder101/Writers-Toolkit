Lesson 19 · Introducing Slam Poetry

Learning Intention & Success Criteria

Learning Intention

We are learning to identify how slam poets use their voice and their words to express powerful ideas.

Success Criteria

  • I can explain what slam poetry is and how it is different from poetry that is only read on a page.
  • I can identify stressed and unstressed syllables in a line of poetry.
  • I can describe how a poet uses rhythm and intonation to create an effect.
  • I can explain, using an example from a performance, how one technique made me think or feel something.

What is slam poetry?

Before we watch anything, get this into your books.

Slam poetry is a type of performance poetry that uses powerful language, rhythm and expression to make people feel or think. It often explores personal stories or big social ideas.

So what makes it different?

A poem on a page is finished when the words are chosen. A slam poem is not finished until somebody says it out loud. The voice is part of the poem.

Written to be heard
Says something that matters
The body is part of it

While you watch

Three things to hold onto. You will need them in a minute.

1. What words or lines stood out?

2. How did the poet speak: fast, slow, loud, soft?

3. What emotions did the poet express?

Eyes up, pens down. You are listening, not writing.

Think, pair, share

Think. On your own. What did you like about the performance? What did you notice the poet doing?

Pair. Turn to the person beside you. Did you notice the same moment, or a different one?

Share. Back to the whole class. Try to name what the poet did, not just that it was good.

Push for the language of the craft. "It was powerful" is a start. "She slowed right down on the last three words so we had to lean in" is the answer we want.

Stressed and unstressed

Stressed syllables are the parts of words we emphasise: louder, longer, more forceful.

Unstressed syllables are the parts we say more softly and quickly.

Every word already has a pattern

Say POE-try. Now try po-E-try. The second one sounds broken, and nobody taught you that rule. Your mouth already knows where the beat goes.

A poet's job is to line those beats up on purpose.

Tap the beats

Say the line out loud, then tap every syllable your voice leans on. LINE 1 OF 3

Rhythm

Rhythm is the pattern of beats created by stressed and unstressed syllables. It gives a poem its musical flow.

Line one had a heartbeat

daDUM daDUM daDUM daDUM

Light, heavy, light, heavy. The pattern of a pulse, which is why it feels steady.

So why break it?

A pattern only has power while it holds. When a poet drops two heavy beats side by side, or stops dead mid line, the room notices. The rhythm is the rule; breaking it is the moment.

Listen again for the line where the poet breaks their own pattern.

Intonation

Intonation is the way the pitch and tone of the voice rises and falls. It carries emotion, adds emphasis, and can change what a line means.

Same six words. Three different poems.

You never saw me coming.

Pick a way to say it, then read it aloud exactly like that.

Your reflection
