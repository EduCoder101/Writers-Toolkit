A biography tells the true story of a real person. Today we read one closely and hunt for the language that makes it work.

What is a biography?

Before we read, let's be clear about what kind of text this is.

⭐ Learn this

The definition

A biography is the true story of a real person's life, written by someone else.

The word comes from Greek: bio means life and graphy means writing. Life-writing.

✏️In your book: write the heading Biography and copy the definition.
⭐ Learn this

Biography or autobiography?

Biography

Written about a person by someone else.

Uses he, she, they and the person's name.

vs

Autobiography

Written by the person about themselves.

Uses I, me, my, we.

Auto means self. An autobiography is self-life-writing.

Passion and courage

Biographers choose people worth writing about. Often that means someone with a passion (something they loved and gave their life to) and courage (the bravery to keep going when it was hard).

As you read about Edmund Hillary, keep two questions in your head:

  • What was his passion?
  • Where do you see his courage?

Formal or informal?

Writers choose a voice to suit the reader and the purpose.

⭐ Learn this

Two ways of writing

Formal

  • Full words, no contractions (did not, not didn't)
  • No slang, no chatty asides
  • Precise, careful vocabulary
  • Mostly third person (he, she, they)
  • Calm, serious tone
vs

Informal

  • Contractions (he's, wasn't)
  • Slang and everyday phrases (a bit of a legend)
  • Talks to the reader (you)
  • Exclamation marks, jokes
  • Sounds like speech

💬 Talk about it

Same fact, two voices

InformalEd was a total legend, honestly. He smashed Everest back in '53 with his mate Tenzing and they only hung around up there for about 15 minutes!
FormalIn 1953, Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reached the summit of Mount Everest. The two men spent around 15 minutes at the highest point on Earth before beginning their descent.

Which one would you trust in a library book? Which one would you say to a friend?

So which voice does a biography use, and why?

A biography is usually written in a formal voice because it needs to be accurate, trusted and respectful of the real person it describes.

Language features of biography

These are the tools biographers reach for. Learn the first four well. The last two are extras.

⭐ Learn this

Past tense verbs

Verbs that tell us something already happened. Most of a biography is in the past tense because the events are over.

In Hillary's story
Hillary was born in Auckland. He joined the tramping club. The two men spent 15 minutes at the summit.
✏️In your book: definition + one example.
⭐ Learn this

Present tense verbs

Verbs for things that are still true now. Biographers switch to the present tense to show that something continues today.

In Hillary's story
Today, he is remembered as the first person to stand on the summit. The Himalayan Trust builds schools and hospitals in Nepal.
✏️In your book: definition + one example. Why is it in the present tense?
⭐ Learn this

Descriptive and emotive language

Words that paint a picture or make the reader feel something. Adjectives, adverbs and strong noun groups do this job.

In Hillary's story
He was a shy, skinny child who spent hours lost in adventure books. The climbers bravely battled thin, freezing air to reach the highly coveted summit.
✏️In your book: definition + one example.
⭐ Learn this

Time connectives

Words and phrases that place events in order. They are the signposts that move the reader through a life.

In Hillary's story
In 1951, Hillary was invited to join an expedition. When the war was over, he became a beekeeper. Two years later, he took part in a British-led attempt.
✏️In your book: definition + two examples.
⭐ Learn this

Classifying language

Nouns that put a person or thing into a group or category: a job, a role, a type.

In Hillary's story
His father was a beekeeper, and his mother was a teacher. He joined the Air Force as a navigator. The Himalayan Trust is a charity.
✏️In your book: definition + one example.
⭐ Learn this

Cause and effect language

Words that show one thing made another thing happen. Look for because, as a result, led to, consequently, so.

In Hillary's story
His mother wanted him to have a good education, so she sent him to Auckland Grammar School. World War II began. As a result, Hillary joined the Air Force. The heavy work led to him becoming a stronger climber.
✏️In your book: definition + one example.

Read the biography

Seven parts of one life. Read it together, then hunt for the features.

Teacher check · number = how many are hidden
Part 1

🌍Who was Sir Edmund Hillary?

Sir Edmund Hillary (1919–2008) was a pioneering mountain climber, explorer and beekeeper from New Zealand. Today, he is remembered as the first person to stand on the summit of Mount Everest, the highest mountain in the world, together with his Nepalese Sherpa guide, Tenzing Norgay. His face still appears on New Zealand's five-dollar note, and people around the world continue to be inspired by his quiet courage.

Part 2

👶Early life

Hillary was born in Auckland, New Zealand, on 20 July 1919. His father, Percival, was a beekeeper, and his mother, Gertrude, was a teacher. He had an older sister, June, and a younger brother, Rex. As a young boy, Edmund grew up on an eight-acre property in the small, sleepy farming village of Tuakau. He was a shy, skinny child who spent hours lost in adventure books, dreaming of faraway places. Because the family kept bees, Edmund learned early what hard, sticky work looked like.

Part 3

🏫Education

Edmund's mother was determined that her son would receive a good education, so she sent him to Auckland Grammar School, a secondary school in the city. Every day for many years, Edmund travelled more than 90 minutes in each direction, by bike and train, just to reach the school gates. He was an average student who finished school with reasonable but unremarkable results. Later, he studied Mathematics and Science at the University of Auckland.

Part 4

⛰️The mountain climbing enthusiast

Hillary's interest in mountain climbing was first sparked at the age of sixteen, in 1935, when he attended a school skiing trip to Mount Ruapehu. The tall, awkward teenager demonstrated remarkable endurance during the trip, far more than his classmates. While he was at university, he joined the tramping club and discovered that he loved the mountains. By the youthful age of twenty, he had already climbed his first major peak, Mount Ollivier, in the wild, icy alps of southern New Zealand.

Part 5

✈️War intervenes

In 1939, World War II began. As a result, Hillary joined the Royal New Zealand Air Force as a navigator. When the war was over, Edmund and his brother followed in the footsteps of their father and became beekeepers. The heavy, outdoor work toughened his body, which led to him becoming an even stronger climber. He continued to climb the highest peaks of his home country, but he also began to set his sights on a far greater challenge: the summit of Mount Everest in the Himalayas.

Part 6

🏔️Conquering Everest

In 1951, Hillary was invited to join a British expedition that explored possible routes up Mount Everest. Two years later, Edmund took part in a British-led attempt to reach the top. At 11:30 in the morning on 29 May 1953, Hillary and Sherpa mountaineer Tenzing Norgay bravely battled thin, freezing air and a 12-metre rock wall to reach the highly coveted summit. Today, that rock wall is known as Hillary's Step. The two men spent around 15 minutes at the highest point on Earth before beginning their dangerous descent. Thanks to this achievement, Hillary was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II only weeks later.

Part 7

🌟Hillary's legacy

Hillary continued exploring later in life, joining expeditions to both the South Pole and the North Pole. Consequently, he received many accolades, including the Order of New Zealand. In 1960, Edmund founded the Himalayan Trust, a charity that builds schools and hospitals in the most isolated parts of Nepal. He devoted much of his life to the Sherpa people because he never forgot who had helped him reach the top. Edmund Hillary died on 11 January 2008, at the age of eighty-eight, in his hometown of Auckland. Today, the Trust still runs, and his story reminds us that ordinary people can do extraordinary things.

Your task: the language treasure hunt

Work in your group of three or four. You need the printed biography, highlighters and a treasure map (the recording sheet).

🤝 Group work

💎 The treasure: what you must find

Six kinds of language are hidden in the text. Each kind has a colour and a target. Highlight every one you find on your group copy, then write your best ones on the sheet.

Past tense verbs find 10
Present tense verbs find 4
Descriptive & emotive find 8

Classifying find 5 · extra

Hit every target and your group has reached the summit. There are more than the targets hidden in the text, so keep hunting if you finish early.

1

Read it aloud

Take turns. One person reads each part while the others follow with a finger. Nobody highlights yet.

2

Hunt

Go back through part by part. Highlight each kind of language in its colour. Talk before you colour: can your group explain why it is that kind?

3

Record

On your treasure map, write out your finds exactly as they appear in the text. Tick off the tally as you go. Then say what job your best example is doing for the reader.

4

Check

When your teacher reveals the answers on the board, tick the ones you found and add any you missed in a different colour. Which kind was hardest to spot?

5

Extra

Hit all four main targets? Go after the extra treasure: classifying language and cause and effect language. There are fewer of these, so look carefully.
