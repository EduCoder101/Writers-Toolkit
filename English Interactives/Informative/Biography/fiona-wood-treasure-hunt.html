Fiona Wood the surgeon who sprays on skin

Name:

Professor Fiona Wood is an inspiring burns surgeon and scientist who invented spray-on skin. Today, her invention helps burns patients in hospitals around the world, and she is known as one of Australia's most trusted scientists.

Fiona was born on 2 February 1958 in a small coal-mining village in Yorkshire, England. Her father was a coal miner and her mother was a PE teacher. As a girl, she dreamed of running in the Olympics, but she chose medicine instead because she wanted to help people. In 1987, she moved to Perth with her husband, Tony, who is also a surgeon.

In 1991, Fiona became Western Australia's first female plastic surgeon. She was frustrated that burns patients had to wait weeks for new skin to grow, so she and scientist Marie Stoner developed a remarkable new idea: growing a patient's own skin cells in a lab and spraying them onto the burn.

In October 2002, bombs exploded in Bali and 28 badly burned survivors were flown to Fiona's hospital in Perth. She and her team worked day and night for weeks, using spray-on skin to save lives. As a result, she was named Australian of the Year in 2005. Fiona still works as a surgeon today, and her foundation raises money for burns research.

Past tense: invented, was born, dreamed, chose, moved, became, developed, were flown, worked, was named
Time connectives: Today, on 2 February 1958, As a girl, In 1987, In 1991, In October 2002, in 2005, today
Present tense: is, helps, is known, still works, raises. The first and last paragraphs are about now, because Fiona is alive and her invention is still in use; the middle is her story so far.
Descriptive & emotive: inspiring, small coal-mining village, frustrated, remarkable, badly burned, day and night. Accept others such as trusted.
Classifying: burns surgeon and scientist, coal miner, PE teacher, plastic surgeon
Cause & effect: because, so, As a result
