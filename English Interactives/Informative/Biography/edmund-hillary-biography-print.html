Sir Edmund Hillary

Group:
Part 1

Who was Sir Edmund Hillary?

Sir Edmund Hillary (1919–2008) was a pioneering mountain climber, explorer and beekeeper from New Zealand. Today, he is remembered as the first person to stand on the summit of Mount Everest, the highest mountain in the world, together with his Nepalese Sherpa guide, Tenzing Norgay. His face still appears on New Zealand's five-dollar note, and people around the world continue to be inspired by his quiet courage.

Part 2

Early life

Hillary was born in Auckland, New Zealand, on 20 July 1919. His father, Percival, was a beekeeper, and his mother, Gertrude, was a teacher. He had an older sister, June, and a younger brother, Rex. As a young boy, Edmund grew up on an eight-acre property in the small, sleepy farming village of Tuakau. He was a shy, skinny child who spent hours lost in adventure books, dreaming of faraway places. Because the family kept bees, Edmund learned early what hard, sticky work looked like.

Part 3

Education

Edmund's mother was determined that her son would receive a good education, so she sent him to Auckland Grammar School, a secondary school in the city. Every day for many years, Edmund travelled more than 90 minutes in each direction, by bike and train, just to reach the school gates. He was an average student who finished school with reasonable but unremarkable results. Later, he studied Mathematics and Science at the University of Auckland.

Part 4

The mountain climbing enthusiast

Hillary's interest in mountain climbing was first sparked at the age of sixteen, in 1935, when he attended a school skiing trip to Mount Ruapehu. The tall, awkward teenager demonstrated remarkable endurance during the trip, far more than his classmates. While he was at university, he joined the tramping club and discovered that he loved the mountains. By the youthful age of twenty, he had already climbed his first major peak, Mount Ollivier, in the wild, icy alps of southern New Zealand.

Part 5

War intervenes

In 1939, World War II began. As a result, Hillary joined the Royal New Zealand Air Force as a navigator. When the war was over, Edmund and his brother followed in the footsteps of their father and became beekeepers. The heavy, outdoor work toughened his body, which led to him becoming an even stronger climber. He continued to climb the highest peaks of his home country, but he also began to set his sights on a far greater challenge: the summit of Mount Everest in the Himalayas.

Part 6

Conquering Everest

In 1951, Hillary was invited to join a British expedition that explored possible routes up Mount Everest. Two years later, Edmund took part in a British-led attempt to reach the top. At 11:30 in the morning on 29 May 1953, Hillary and Sherpa mountaineer Tenzing Norgay bravely battled thin, freezing air and a 12-metre rock wall to reach the highly coveted summit. Today, that rock wall is known as Hillary's Step. The two men spent around 15 minutes at the highest point on Earth before beginning their dangerous descent. Thanks to this achievement, Hillary was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II only weeks later.

Part 7

Hillary's legacy

Hillary continued exploring later in life, joining expeditions to both the South Pole and the North Pole. Consequently, he received many accolades, including the Order of New Zealand. In 1960, Edmund founded the Himalayan Trust, a charity that builds schools and hospitals in the most isolated parts of Nepal. He devoted much of his life to the Sherpa people because he never forgot who had helped him reach the top. Edmund Hillary died on 11 January 2008, at the age of eighty-eight, in his hometown of Auckland. Today, the Trust still runs, and his story reminds us that ordinary people can do extraordinary things.
