When, Where, HowStart your sentences somewhere new.

We are learning to vary how our sentences start, with time, place and manner openers, while keeping events connected.

10 minutes: watch20 minutes: rebuild6 paragraphs to choose from
Success criteria
  • I can spot the opener at the start of a sentence.
  • I can start a sentence with a When, Where or How opener and a comma.
  • I can link a sentence back to the one before.
  • I can explain how my opener helps the reader.

Three openers you already own

You already know adverbials that answer When? Where? and How? Today we move them to the front of the sentence.

When?

Time opener · T
At midnight,Moments later,After school,As the sun set,
At midnight, I heard a faint tapping.

Where?

Place opener · P
Beyond the gate,Inside the cave,Far below,Behind her,
Beyond the window, a shadow moved.

How?

Manner opener · M
Carefully,Without a sound,On tiptoe,Heart pounding,
Without a sound, I crept to the door.
The comma
Beyond the window, a shadow moved.
In your book

Rule three columns: When (T) Where (P) How (M). Copy two openers into each one.

Learn this

An opener comes first, then a comma, then the rest of the sentence. The comma tells the reader: here comes the main part.

  • Link to the adverbial phrases lesson: these are the same When, Where and How phrases, just moved to the front.
  • T, P and M (time, place, manner) are the codes students write above each opener in their rebuild.
  • One click per opener. Read each example aloud and ask: what does the reader know now that they did not before?
  • “Heart pounding” is a How opener that shows a feeling without naming it. Your class will recognise that from show, don't tell.

5 · I do2 minWatch

Link it back

The best openers do two jobs. They tell the reader when or where, and they point back to what just happened.

So farAt dusk, Lily opened the gate. Beyond it, a narrow path wound into the trees. Carefully, she stepped forward.
Flat versionLily heard footsteps. Lily heard a twig snap. Lily saw her little brother.
Moments later, footsteps crunched on the path.Later than what? Later than the moment she stepped forward.
Behind her, a twig snapped.Behind who? Behind Lily. We already know where she is standing.
By the gate, her little brother was grinning from ear to ear.Which gate? The one from the very first sentence.
Learn this

A linking opener is a hook that joins this sentence to one before it. The paragraph reads as one chain, not a list of separate sentences.

In your book

Under your columns, write Link it back and copy three linking openers: Beyond it, Moments later, Behind her,

Linking words

it · her · them · later · there · the same place again

  • The program's version uses “Moments later, I sprinted towards the gate. Behind me, the footsteps grew louder.” This version carries on Lily's story from card 4 and ends on a smile rather than a chase.
  • Each click lights up one opener and the part of the earlier text it points back to.
  • Ask after each one: “Later than what?” “Behind who?” “Which gate?” If the reader can answer, the sentence is linked.
  • This is cohesion: the program's “keeping events connected”.
6 · I do1 minSpot

Too much of a good thing

Every sentence has an opener
At dusk, Lily opened the gate. Slowly, she walked down the path. Carefully, she looked around. Quietly, she listened.

A new pattern: opener, comma, she. Opener, comma, she. Opener, comma, she.

A mix
At dusk, Lily opened the gate. She walked slowly down the path, peering into the shadows. Somewhere ahead, an owl hooted.
Learn this

Aim for a mix. Some sentences start with an opener. Some start the plain way. Different lengths help too.

Watch out

Then and Suddenly are the openers everyone overuses. Use them once, if at all.

In your book

Write: Mix it up. Not every sentence needs an opener.

  • Some students overcorrect and put an opener on every sentence. This card heads that off before they start.
  • Click 1: read the top version aloud in a sing-song rhythm so they hear the new pattern. Three “-ly” words in a row is also a giveaway.
  • Click 2: the fix keeps two openers, lets one sentence start plainly and changes the sentence lengths.

8 · Your turn1 minChoose

Choose a flat paragraph

Six places nobody has been before, and every paragraph keeps starting the same way. Pick the place you can picture best.

Learn this

Your paragraph is not wrong. It is flat. Your job is to rebuild it so it flows.

In your book

Write your paragraph's letter and title, for example C: The Glowing Cave.

  • All six paragraphs follow the program's prompt: a character discovers an unfamiliar place.
  • Each one repeats a different start: I, We, Zara, Then, The and She. D (Then) and E (The) are a little trickier, because the repetition is not a person.
  • Thirty seconds to choose. Fast finishers can do a second paragraph.
9 · You write13 minIn your book

Rebuild your paragraph

Writing time
13:00
Press Start when every title is written.
Paragraph
Floor
Start on any floor. It is where you finish that matters.
The three floors
  • Entry: three openers ready-made under every sentence. Students tap to try each one, choose, and copy into their books. Nudge them to make one opener their own by changing a word.
  • First floor: the paragraph with When, Where and How prompts, and the big opener bank.
  • Second floor: no bank. Four challenges, including linking back and a How opener that shows a feeling.
  • Everyone does the same core task. Students can switch floors at any time.
While they write
  • The timer calls out at five minutes and at one minute. If you change cards it keeps running in the top bar.
  • “Show me how to start” reveals the first two sentences of one writer's version. The full versions are on card 11, for after the writing.
  • Circulate with one question: “Does this sentence link back to the one before?”
  • Watch for commas missing after openers, and for a new pattern creeping in (opener, comma, she, every time).

Coach stems
  • Sentence ___ could start with a When opener.
  • You need a comma after ___.
  • Sentences ___ and ___ both start with ___.

In your book

Check time
3:00
