Step 1Where does your story happen?

Pick one place. Every pebble, rock and boulder you see next is made for that place, so your ladder fits together.

If you invent your own place, you invent your own pebble, rock and boulder too.
For teachers
  • Planning, about 15 to 20 minutes: steps 1 to 8. Fewer, tighter choices than the Story Ladder Builder: 8 places, 3 pebbles for each place, 2 rocks for each pebble and 2 boulders for each rock, so every chain passes the because test.
  • Writing, about 40 minutes: one card for each of the 5 paragraphs. Each card shows that part of the plan, then pairs of simple sentences written for the exact events the student chose.
  • Students join a pair in their book with a tension word (when, while, as, because, although), then press Check to compare it with a model and the flipped version. This follows on from Wait for it.
  • Strong writers can skip the pairs and write their own complex sentences. The tension word tracker and the final check still apply.
  • Choices save on this computer, so students can carry on next lesson. Arrow keys move between steps.

Step 2Who is your main character?

A name, one strength and one worry. Be quick: your writing time matters more.

One strength

What are they good at?

One worry

What are they secretly scared of?

Step 3The pebble: what goes wrong first?

A small problem. Annoying, but fixable.

Step 4The rock: what happens because of that?

A bigger problem that grows out of the pebble. Is more worried now? Does “because of that” make sense?

Step 5The boulder: the biggest problem of all

Think first. A good boulder passes all three checks.

1
It is the hardest problem so far. It looks almost impossible.
2
It happens because of the rock.
3
Something cares about is at risk: safety, a friend, a dream or a promise.

Step 6The climax: how does beat the boulder?

Your main character is the hero. No dreams, no grown-up rescues, no “then we went home and had dinner”.

Step 7How does your story end?

Show what has learned or how they have changed.

Step 8Your story plan

In your book

Write your title. Jot your plan as five quick dot points: pebble, rock, boulder, climax, ending. Keep it short. Your writing time matters more.

Ready to write?

You will write one paragraph at a time. Each one has short sentences for you to join into complex sentences that make the reader wait.

Last stepCheck your story

Read your whole story quietly to yourself. Does the reader have to wait?

In your book

Underline every dependent clause. Ring every comma after a dependent clause that comes first. Then tick what you can see.

Finished early?

Find the tensest sentence in your story. Flip its dependent clause to the other end. Write both versions under your story, labelled Before and After. Which one makes the reader wait longer?

My journey map

Every idea you were offered is faded. The bright path is the one you chose. Tap a step to go back to it.

Clear your whole story?

This removes every choice you have made and every idea you have typed, on every step. You cannot undo it.

If you are only changing one thing, go back to that step instead.
