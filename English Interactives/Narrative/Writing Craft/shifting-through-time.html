Year 5 Narrative · Writing

Shifting Through Time

Writers are time travellers. Today we learn how to jump forward, slow down, and skip the dull bits, and how one small connective can change the whole feel of a story.

What we are learning

Use time to control the reader

We are learning to use time connectives to move our reader through a narrative, and to control its pace and mood.

A time connective is a signpost that tells the reader we have moved to a new moment in time.

I can explain why writers shift through time instead of describing every moment.

I can spot a time connective and name the kind of time shift it signals.

I can choose connectives to speed a story up or slow it down, and write using at least three.

01 · The big idea 5 min

A story is not real time

If you wrote down every single second of a character's day, your reader would fall asleep before the character even got out of bed.

Real life runs second by second. A good story does not. The writer chooses which moments to stretch out and which to skip past. Time connectives are how we move between those moments without the reader getting lost.

Without them, writing lurches and jolts. With them, the reader glides.

Jolting Tom finished breakfast. He brushed his teeth. He walked to school. He sat down. The bell rang. The teacher spoke. It was lunchtime.
Gliding Tom finished breakfast. Soon after, he was walking to school. By the time the bell rang, he had almost forgotten the strange letter. At lunchtime, he remembered.

02 · How they work 10 min

The four jobs a connective can do

Every time connective is really doing one of four jobs. Once you know the job, you know which one to reach for.

Set the moment

Opens a scene and tells us when we are.

In the morningWhen the sun sankOn the first sunny dayAt night

A small step

Nudges the story on a little, minutes or hours.

AfterwardsSoon afterAfter lunchLaterThe next day

A big leap

Jumps a long way: weeks, months, years.

A week laterMonths passedThe following TuesdayIn the year 2004

At the same time

Shows two things happening together.

MeanwhileAt the same timeAs they approached

Read a connective from the handout aloud and have the class call out its job: set the moment, small step, big leap, or same time?

03 · Model 8 min

The secret is the gap

A time connective tells the reader how big the gap between two events feels. Keep the events the same, change the size of the gaps, and the whole mood changes. Here is one set of events, written three ways.

The events: the phone buzzed → she read the message → she reached for her coat

Calm

After lunch, the phone buzzed. A little while later, she read the message. Soon after, she reached for her coat.

Gentle, even gaps. An ordinary afternoon, nothing rushing.

Frantic

All at once, the phone buzzed. In an instant, she read the message. Before she could think, she had reached for her coat.

Tiny gaps. The events pile up and the reader is rushed along.

Eerie

At last, the phone buzzed. After what felt like hours, she read the message. A long time passed before she reached for her coat.

Long, uneven gaps. Time drags and starts to feel strange.

Read all three aloud. The words in bold are the only thing that changed.

04 · Explore 12 min

Same story, different mood

Maya does exactly the same four things every time. We change only the time connectives. Watch what happens to the feeling of the story.

In the morning, Maya stepped out of the house.

Soon after, she reached the old stone bridge.

Later that evening, a figure appeared at the far end.

The next day, she was back behind her own front door.

SlowFast

Ask the class: which single connective changed the feeling the most? Could you make this story funny, or boring on purpose?

05 · Model the write 8 min

How to build a story

Everyone can start from the same events. The connectives you choose are what make the story yours. Here is how to turn a list of events into a story with a mood.

1

Start with the bones

The same events, in order. These do not change.

  1. Maya found a small brass key.
  2. She unlocked the old door.
  3. She climbed the narrow stairs.
  4. She reached the room at the top.
  5. The light went out.
  6. She found her way home.
2

Choose a mood

For this one, let's build it eerie

3

Add connectives that fit the mood

At last, Maya found a small brass key.
When the sun had sunk, she unlocked the old door.
After what felt like hours, she climbed the narrow stairs.
A long time passed before she reached the room at the top.
Without warning, the light went out.
Much later, she found her way home.
4

Write it out, and add a little detail of your own

The finished story At last, Maya found a small brass key, half-buried in the dust. When the sun had sunk behind the hills, she unlocked the old door. After what felt like hours, she climbed the narrow stairs, one slow step at a time. A long time passed before she reached the room at the top. Without warning, the light went out. Much later, cold and shaking, she found her way home.

This is exactly the task on the sheet: the same six events for everyone, different connectives for everyone.

06 · Your turn 18 min

Now you control the clock

Pick one route. Use the handout as your toolbox. Keep it short: this is about controlling time, not writing a whole chapter.

A Steady support

Build the mood

Choose a mood, then choose connectives to match it.

  1. Choose a mood: calm, frantic or eerie.
  2. Fill each blank with a connective that fits your mood.
  3. Read it back. Does it feel the way you wanted?
the door swung open.
the footsteps grew louder.
the lights went out.
everything was still.

Then write your version out in full sentences on the sheet.

B Ready to fly

Free write

Write a short narrative of your own.

  • Write about 6 to 10 sentences.
  • Move the reader through time at least three times.
  • Include at least one big leap (a week, a month, years).
  • Make sure your connectives match the mood you want.

Idea starters: a journey, a long wait, a day that goes wrong, a secret kept over many years.

07 · Reflect 5 min

Before we finish

  • When would a writer want to slow time down? When would they want to speed it up?
  • Find one time connective in your writing. Swap it for a different one. How did the feeling change?
  • Share the connective you are most proud of, and the job it was doing.
