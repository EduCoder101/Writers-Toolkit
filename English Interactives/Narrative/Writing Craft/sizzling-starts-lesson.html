Learning Intention

We are learning to start a story where something is already happening, so the reader cannot help but keep reading.

Success Criteria

  • I can name the four ways to sizzle and spot them in other writers' openings.
  • I can describe a sound instead of spelling it out in capital letters.
  • I can bring in a sense (sight, smell, sound, taste, touch or balance) in my first lines.
  • I can rewrite a boring opening as a sizzling start, and explain which way I used.
  • I never open with waking up, the weather, or "One day".
Hook

Which one would you keep reading?

Two writers were given the same story. Read both openings.

Opening A
One day Mia woke up. She got dressed and went downstairs and had some breakfast. It was a sunny day.
Opening B
The smoke alarm screamed. Mia was halfway down the stairs before she remembered the cake.
Talk to your partner
Which opening would you keep reading? What has the writer of Opening B done that the writer of Opening A has not? Be ready to share one idea.
The big idea
Opening B starts where something is already happening. We hear the alarm. We are on the stairs with Mia. We want to know about that cake. Opening A has not started yet. Nothing is happening.
In your book
Write the date and the title Sizzling Starts. Underneath, copy this: A sizzling start begins where something is already happening.

Learn this
There are four ways to sizzle: Action, Dialogue, Sound and Strong feeling. Each one drops the reader into a moment that is already moving.
Anchor chart

Four ways to sizzle

1. Action
The character is doing something fast or risky.
2. Dialogue
Someone is speaking, often urgently.
3. Sound
A sound, described so we can hear it.
4. Strong feeling
We are inside the character's emotion.
The secret ingredient: the senses
Sight, smell, sound, taste, touch, and even balance. Any of the four ways gets stronger when the reader can sense the moment.
In your book
Write the four ways as a numbered list: 1. Action 2. Dialogue 3. Sound 4. Strong feeling. Add a fifth line: Secret ingredient: the senses.
Sound

Describe the sound. Don't shout it.

CRASH! BANG! SPLASH! These are the easy way out. A real writer tells us what the sound was actually like. Try each one with your partner before you click.

CRASH!
→
The crunch of metal folding in on itself, then a tinkling of glass across the road.
BANG!
→
The classroom door slammed so hard the windows rattled in their frames.
SPLASH!
→
The water swallowed my phone with a soft, final plop, and the pool went quiet.
SCREECH!
→
A chair scraped slowly across the tiles behind me. Nobody else was meant to be in the room.
Learn this
Sound words to reach for: crunch scrape rattle splinter hiss thud groan creak hum crackle. Then say what made the sound and what it did to the silence.
In your book
Next to 3. Sound write: describe it, don't shout it.
The secret ingredient

Use the senses

The reader has a body too. Give them something to see, smell, hear, taste, touch, or feel in their balance, and they are standing inside your story. Click each sense to see one way in.

Sight
Click for an example
Every sleeping bag was zipped up. Every one was empty.
Smell
Click for an example
The smell hit me first: burnt sugar, and something sharper underneath it.
Sound
Click for an example
The bookshelf groaned, and then it swung open.
Taste
Click for an example
The pool water tasted of chlorine and panic.
Touch
Click for an example
The rope burned through my palms, and the creek rushed up to meet me.
Balance
Click for an example
The floor tilted. I grabbed the bench, and the bench moved too.
Look again
Which of the four ways is each of these using as well? (The balance one is also action. The smell one is also a strong feeling, because something is wrong.) The senses do not replace the four ways. They make them stronger.

Quick fire

Sizzle or fizzle?

Thumbs up for sizzle, thumbs down for fizzle. If it sizzles, which way is it? Click each one to check.

01
"Run," Isla whispered, "and don't look at the windows."
Sizzle: dialogue
Someone is speaking urgently. We are already in trouble.
02
It was a cold and windy morning when I got up for school.
Fizzle
Weather and waking up. Two rules broken in one sentence.
03
Something scratched at the inside of the cupboard door. Slowly. Patiently.
Sizzle: sound
A described sound, and nobody shouted SCRATCH.
04
One day a girl called Ruby went to a place that was very interesting.
Fizzle
"One day", and nothing is happening. Even the place is a mystery to the writer.
05
My cheeks were on fire before she had even finished reading out the scores.
Sizzle: strong feeling
Embarrassment, felt in the body. We do not need to be told she was embarrassed.
06
I was over the fence and halfway across the paddock before I let myself breathe.
Sizzle: action
We arrive mid-sprint. Why is she running? We have to keep reading.
Story idea 1 of 4
Fantasy / Mystery
The Door Behind the Library Shelf
A hidden door appears in the school library that only you can see.
A boring start
One day I went to the library to return my book. It was quiet. Then I noticed a door I had not seen before.
A sizzling start
The bookshelf groaned, and then it swung open.
SoundAction
A described sound (groaned), and the shelf is moving before we know why.
Your turn, out loud
How could you start this story with dialogue? With a strong feeling? Tell your partner one sentence.
Story idea 2 of 4
Realistic / Sport
The Night Before the Final
The night before the state final (dance, netball, gymnastics, swimming, you choose), everything starts going wrong.
A boring start
Tomorrow was the big final. I was feeling a bit nervous. I went to my room to pack my bag.
A sizzling start
My costume was gone. Not misplaced. Gone.
Strong feelingSight
Three short sentences. The panic is in the rhythm, not in the word "nervous".
Your turn, out loud
Start this one with a sound. What could you hear the night before a final? Tell your partner.
Story idea 3 of 4
Fantasy / Friendship
The Friendship Bracelet
A bracelet from a market stall lets you hear what your best friend is really thinking.
A boring start
On Saturday Mum took me to the markets. I bought a bracelet. It was nice and it had blue beads.
A sizzling start
I heard her voice clearly, but her lips never moved.
Sound and sight
Two senses that do not agree. The reader's brain has to solve it, so it keeps reading.
Your turn, out loud
Start this one with dialogue. What is the first thing you hear your best friend think? Tell your partner.
Story idea 4 of 4
Adventure / Suspense
Lost on School Camp
On the last night of camp, you step outside your cabin and every other cabin is empty.
A boring start
I woke up in the middle of the night on camp. It was dark and cold. I looked around for my friends.
A sizzling start
Every sleeping bag was zipped up. Every one was empty.
SightStrong feeling
The boring start broke two rules. The sizzling start just shows us what she sees, and lets us feel the dread ourselves.
Your turn, out loud
Start this one with action or a sound. The cabins are empty. What do you do first? What do you hear?

We do

Boring start makeover

In your book
Rewrite each boring opening as a sizzling start. Use a different way each time and write the way you used in the margin. Bring in at least one sense. Star your favourite.
MAKEOVER 1
We got on the bus to go to school camp. It was a long drive.
One way it could go
The bus lurched around the bend and every bag in the overhead rack slid six inches to the left. Somebody's lunchbox burst open. We had not even left the car park.
ActionSound and balance
MAKEOVER 2
On Saturday I had my dance competition. I was a bit nervous.
One way it could go
My legs had forgotten how to be legs. The music started, and for one terrible second I could not remember a single step.
Strong feelingTouch and sound
MAKEOVER 3
My friend gave me a present. It was a bracelet.
One way it could go
"Don't put it on yet," Ava said, glancing at the door. "Not until you're home. Promise me."
DialogueSight
MAKEOVER 4
I went to the library at lunchtime to find a book.
One way it could go
Somewhere behind the history shelves, a book slid out and hit the floor with a flat, heavy slap. The library was supposed to be empty.

You do

Partner check

Swap books. Read your partner's opening twice. Then write three things in the margin of their book.

1. Name the way
Which of the four ways did they use? Write it. If you cannot tell, write "Which way?" and talk about it.
2. Find the sense
Underline the words that let you see, smell, hear, taste, touch or feel the moment. If there are none, write "Sense?"
3. The question
Write the question the opening made you ask. ("Why is she running?" "What is behind the door?") That question is the reason a reader keeps going.
In your book
Get your book back. Read your partner's three notes. Change one thing in your opening to make it sizzle harder.
