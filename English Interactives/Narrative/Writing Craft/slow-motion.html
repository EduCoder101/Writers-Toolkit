Learn this

Slow motion means more words, less time. In Version B, Lily has not even taken a step yet.

In your book

Nothing to write yet. Talk to your partner, then listen.

  • Read Version A flatly, then ask how much time passes. Take two or three guesses before you click.
  • Read Version B slowly, leaning on every full stop. Ask the same question again.
  • Land the comparison: Version B has nearly three times the words and covers about ten seconds. Lily has not even started walking.
  • Lily, Ava and the creek come from the camp story the class has already met.

4 · I do2 minWatch

Watch me stretch it

Fast versionI waited for my toast to pop.7 words
Slow-motion version Each click adds one sentence. Guess the tool before it appears.

The toaster ticked on the bench. I stood in front of it in my socks, the kitchen tiles icy under my feet. Through the slots, the wires glowed orange.

Then the smell arrived: warm and nutty, with a tiny edge of burnt. My stomach growled so loudly that I checked the doorway in case anyone had heard.

Don’t touch it, I told myself. A watched toaster never pops.

I leaned closer. A single crumb trembled on the edge of the slot. My fingers hovered over the lever. The ticking slowed.

Any second now.

Learn this

The toast never pops. 93 words, about ten seconds, and almost nothing happened. You still wanted to know what came next. That is slow motion.

5 · Your turn1 minListen

The Slow-Motion Challenge

  1. 1
    Pick one scene. Stay in it.You will choose from three on the next card.
  2. 2
    Stay inside the moment.No “later”, no “after a while”, no “the next day”. Every sentence happens in the same few seconds.
  3. 3
    The big thing must not happen.Your scene tells you what it is. Hold it off until your very last line, or never let it happen at all.
  4. 4
    Show, don't tell.No “I was scared”, “I was nervous” or “I was excited”. Let your body and your thoughts show it.
  5. 5
    Use all five tools.Tick them down the side of your page as you go.

The longest piece of writing that works wins.

What counts?Only sentences that work. At the end, your partner crosses out any sentence that breaks a rule. Then you count the words that are left.
Learn this

Long is easy. Long and slow is the skill.

In your book

Nothing to write yet. Listen to the rules.

  • One click per rule. Rule 3 is the heart of it: once the big thing happens, the moment is over, so it never happens, or only in the very last line.
  • “That works” is what stops a list of random sentences from winning. The partner check on card 8 enforces it.
6 · Choose30 secChoose

Pick your scene

AThe Diving Board

You are standing at the very end of the high diving board at the swimming carnival. Your toes curl over the edge. The whole school is watching.

Must not happen: you jump.

BThe Last Drip

It is 38 degrees at the beach. A drip of melting ice cream is sliding down your cone, heading straight for your fingers.

Must not happen: it reaches your hand.

CThe Surprise

The lights are off. You are squashed behind the couch with twenty people, waiting to shout “Surprise!” A key scrapes in the front door.

Must not happen: the lights come on.
Learn this

Choose the moment you can picture most clearly. You will be living inside it for fifteen minutes.

In your book

Write your scene's name as your title, for example The Last Drip.

  • Give 30 seconds to choose. No swapping once the timer starts.
  • For anyone who cannot decide: the ice cream is the easiest way in, the diving board has the most tension, and the surprise has the most sounds.

Check: has the big thing happened? It must not. Not yet.
Stuck? A · Diving Board
  • What does the board feel like under your toes?
  • What can you see when you look straight down?
  • Who can you spot in the crowd? What are they doing?
  • What are your stomach, knees and hands doing?
  • What are you telling yourself?
Stuck? B · Last Drip
  • How fast is the drip moving? What colour is it?
  • What does the sun feel like on the back of your neck?
  • What can you hear and smell at the beach?
  • Is anything else interested in your ice cream?
  • What are you thinking: lick it now, or not yet?
Stuck? C · Surprise
  • What can you make out in the dark?
  • Who is squashed next to you? What can you smell?
  • What does the carpet feel like under your knees?
  • What can you hear on the other side of the door?
  • What are you trying very hard not to do?
Sentence starters
For a second, …Somewhere behind me, …Out of the corner of my eye, …I could feel …My ___ would not stop ___.I told myself, …Even closer, I noticed …Not yet.
  • Start the timer and keep the room silent for the first five minutes.
  • The timer calls out at five minutes and at one minute. When students run dry, point to the loop: go round the senses, the body and the thoughts again, closer each time.
  • Circulate with one question: “Has the big thing happened yet?”
  • The scene questions and sentence starters stay closed. Open one for a student who is stuck, not for the whole room.
  • If you change cards, the timer keeps running and shows in the top bar. Tap it to come back.
8 · Count up4 minPartners

Count what works

1Pens down

Finish the sentence you are on. Do not end the moment.

2Swap books

Read your partner's writing quietly, all the way through.

3Cross out

One light pencil line through any sentence that breaks a rule: it skips ahead, names a feeling, or lets the big thing happen.

4Count what works

Count the words that are left. Put a small dot every ten words so you do not lose your place.

5Top three read

The highest counts read aloud. The class decides: does every sentence stay inside the moment?

Longest that works winsLong is easy. Long and slow is the skill.

Learn this

Writers slow down the moments that matter most. Next, you will slow down the climax of your own story with at least two tools.

In your book

Write your word count at the bottom of your writing and circle it.

  • Partners cross out in light pencil, so the writer can see what broke a rule: a jump forward in time, a named feeling, or the big thing happening early.
  • A dot every ten words keeps the counting quick and checkable.
  • The top three read aloud. The class judges whether every sentence stays inside the moment, which is the real success criterion.
  • Link forward: the Lesson 7 draft checklist asks for the climax slowed down with two or more tools. Today was the practice for that.
