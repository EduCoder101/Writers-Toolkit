How Setting Develops Theme

Learning Intention

To explore how setting develops theme in a novel.

Success Criteria · I can…

  • describe how the setting creates challenges
  • connect challenges to a theme
  • explain how the author uses language devices in the setting to shape meaning
I DO watch & listen

First, what is a setting detail?

A setting detail is one specific thing about where or when a story takes place. Not the whole place, just one piece you could point to: the weather, an object, a smell, a single room.

Authors choose their setting details on purpose. A strong setting detail does a job. It creates a challenge for the characters, and that challenge helps a theme grow.

The pattern we are looking for

Setting detail → Challenge → Theme

A setting detail creates a challenge, and the challenge develops a theme.

Let's model the thinking: The Explorer

I will think aloud. We start with one setting detail, ask what challenge it creates, then ask what theme that grows.

Setting detail

The dense jungle, thick with tangled vines.

↓  so what challenge does that create?
Challenge

It is exhausting to push through, and very easy to get lost.

↓  and what theme does that grow?
Theme

Perseverance. The children must keep going when everything is hard.

The same pattern works in any book

This is not just an Amazon trick. The exact same thinking works in stories you already know.

Harry Potter
Setting detail

The Forbidden Forest, dark and full of unknown creatures.

↓
Challenge

The characters have to go in, even though they are frightened.

↓
Theme

Courage, and facing your fears.

Charlie and the Chocolate Factory
Setting detail

A room made of chocolate, with a flowing chocolate river and sweets you can eat.

↓
Challenge

The children are tempted to grab and gobble everything in sight.

↓
Theme

Greed, and the consequences of not controlling it.

Time markers in narratives

Narratives are usually chronological, but authors deliberately stretch, skip and squeeze time to build tension. Reveal a few examples:

›“The next day…”
›“Meanwhile…”
›“A full twelve days since the crash…”

What Happened Next?

Learning Intention

To develop character voice, tension and pacing when writing a narrative chapter.

Success Criteria · I can…

  • develop believable character reactions and dialogue
  • vary my pacing to create suspense
  • edit my writing to improve clarity and impact
I DO watch & listen

Reread the final chapter. What might the next scene be for a particular character and their family? Let's build a model together, one part at a time. Imagine Fred is reunited with his father…

Fred stepped off the boat and froze. His father stood waiting near the dock, his tie crooked and his sleeves rolled up as if he had rushed straight from work. For a moment, neither of them spoke. Then his father strode forward and pulled Fred into a tight hug. “…if I’d lost you, Fred… if I’d lost you…” he said quietly, his voice shaking. Fred stared in surprise. He couldn’t remember the last time his father had hugged him, or spoken so freely.

How a writer lifts a basic paragraph

›Add sensory details.
›Replace simple verbs with precise vocabulary.
›Include internal thoughts.
›Vary your sentence length.
YOU DO your turn

Write the chapter that follows the ending for one character. Focus on characterisation and suspense. You may choose to:

Becoming a writer
A scene where a child begins to write their story (the novel).
A new conflict
Conflict between the character and someone newly introduced, for example Con and her aunt.
A new setting
Describe a new setting for the character, such as their home.

Editing checklist (tap each one as you check it)

0 / 4 checks complete

  • Vocabulary: have I chosen precise, vivid words?
  • Dialogue: does it sound like the character and is it punctuated correctly?
  • Paragraphing: have I started a new paragraph for new ideas and speakers?
  • Tension: have I varied my pacing to build suspense?

The Job Application

Learning Intention

To write a formal persuasive text by adopting a character's voice and applying knowledge of their traits.

Success Criteria · I can…

  • identify key character traits and use them to inform my writing
  • write in a formal register appropriate to a job application
  • use persuasive language to present a character's skills convincingly
  • structure a job application with an opening, evidence of skills and a closing statement

Model: Lila applies to be a zookeeper

Lila cared for injured animals throughout the story, stayed calm under pressure and was deeply observant. Let's reveal her application part by part, and notice the formal register.

Opening: I am writing to apply for the position of Zookeeper at your wildlife park. I am a calm, observant and deeply committed carer of animals.

Skill + evidence: During my time in the Amazon, I demonstrated a lifelong commitment to animal welfare. I treated injured creatures with patience and care, often under extremely difficult conditions.

Skill + evidence: I have shown that I remain calm under pressure. When others panicked, I observed each animal closely and responded sensibly.

Closing: I believe I am the ideal candidate because few applicants have cared for wild animals in the rainforest itself. I would be honoured to bring this experience to your team.
Formal registerWe do not write “I really love animals.” We write “I have demonstrated a lifelong commitment to animal welfare.”
WE DO let's try together

In pairs, choose one character and draft the opening paragraph of their application. As you write, ask: which specific moment from the story proves this skill, and how do I say that formally?

YOU DO your turn

Plan first

Before you write, plan:
  • Who am I applying to, and for what role?
  • What are my 3 strongest skills?
  • What evidence from the Amazon proves each skill?
  • Why am I the best person for this job?

Then write: opening → skill + evidence (x3) → closing statement

Sentence starters:
  • I am writing to apply for…
  • During my expedition to the Amazon, I demonstrated…
  • I believe I am the ideal candidate because…
Extension
The employer replies
Write the employer's response: either offer the job with specific reasons, or reject the application and explain what was missing.
