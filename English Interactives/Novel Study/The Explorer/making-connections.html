Year 5 Novel Study · The Explorer

Making Connections

Good readers do not just follow the story. They link it to their own lives, to other texts, to the wider world and to the media around them. Today we connect to grow our understanding.

Learning Intention & Success Criteria ▼

Learning Intention

To make different types of connections to a text to deepen our understanding of characters, themes and ideas.

Success Criteria

  • I can identify different types of connections.
  • I can explain how a connection helps me understand the text.
  • I can support my ideas with evidence from The Explorer.
5 min Why readers connect

A connection is a bridge

When something in a book reminds you of something you already know, you have built a bridge between the page and your own thinking. That bridge is where understanding grows. The more bridges you build, the deeper you read.

There are four kinds of bridge we can build. Each one links the text to a different place.

+

Text to Self

It reminds me of my own life and experiences.

+

Text to Text

It reminds me of another book, film, song or picture.

+

Text to World

It reminds me of real events and ideas in the world.

+

Text to Media

It reminds me of adverts, the news, TV or videos online.

15 min We do · Part A

Connect the crash landing

Look back at the opening of The Explorer: the plane crash and being lost in the Amazon. In your group, make a connection of each type to these early scenes. There are two ways to run this, so open the one your teacher has chosen below.

The opening scenes: sudden loss of control, then survival in a place far from home.

Place the four signs around the room. In groups of four, move from sign to sign. Every member writes at least one connection on a post-it note and adds it under the matching sign. Read each other's notes before you add your own, then be ready to share your group's boldest connection.

Text to Self

A time you felt scared, lost or far from home.

Text to Text

Another survival or adventure story, film or game.

Text to World

A real situation where people lost control of what happens next.

Text to Media

A news story, documentary or advert about survival or safety.

Give each table group a sheet of A3. Fold or rule it into four quadrants and label each one with a connection type. As a group, talk through the opening scenes and write your connections straight into the matching quadrant. Pick a scribe, or pass the pen around so everyone adds at least one.

Text to Self

A time you felt scared, lost or far from home.

Text to Text

Another survival or adventure story, film or game.

Text to World

A real situation where people lost control of what happens next.

Text to Media

A news story, documentary or advert about survival or safety.

Push your thinking

  • Text to World: the crash signals a loss of control and deep uncertainty. What real events feel uncertain in that same way? Think climate change, war, or a sudden change no one chose.
  • Text to Media: reach past the obvious. The Thai cave rescue, where a soccer team was trapped and saved, or an airline safety film, both connect to being trapped and to staying calm in danger.
Model connection
Text to World: the children cannot control the crash. This reminds me of the news when a flood forces families to leave home with no warning. It helps me understand how powerless the characters feel.
20 min You do · Part B

Your own connection

  1. Listen as we read The Vow together. It is short, so it works as our shared connection chapter.
  2. Choose a chapter to work from: The Vow, Max, or a chapter of your own that you know well.
  3. Open the connection task and make one strong connection of each type, using evidence from your chapter.
  4. Finish every connection by explaining what it helps you understand about a character, theme or idea.
