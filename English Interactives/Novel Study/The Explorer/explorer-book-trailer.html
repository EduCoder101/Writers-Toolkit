Year 5 Novel Study · The Explorer by Katherine Rundell

Make a Book Trailer

Watch how trailers tempt a reader in, work out what makes them land, then plan and film a 30 to 60 second trailer that sells The Explorer to readers in other classes.

Learning intention

We are learning how the choices a film-maker makes (image, sound, text, pace) create mood and tempt a reader, so we can plan a short trailer that promotes a novel without giving away its secrets.

I can

  • name the elements that make a book trailer work
  • plan a 30 to 60 second trailer with a hook and a clear mood
  • choose images, sound and words that match the book's tone
  • tease the story without spoiling the ending

03 Success criteria · co-construct

What makes a good book trailer?

Pull your ideas together. A strong trailer usually does these five things.

01

A hook

Something in the opening seconds that grabs attention and makes a viewer keep watching.

02

Theme & atmosphere

Features that reflect the book's themes and mood, so the trailer feels like the book.

03

No spoilers

Enough insight into the story to intrigue, without revealing the twists or the ending.

04

Matched choices

Text, voiceover, images and music all chosen to reflect the tone and intent of the book.

05

A sense of audience

Clues to who the book is for, so the right readers know straight away that this one is for them.

05 Plan it · storyboard first

Map your trailer before you film

Use a storyboard (Google Slides or Canva work well) to plan your scenes: the opening hook, your key images or phrases, and where the mood shifts.

Mood shift · Danger

Danger

A crashed plane, dense jungle closing in, a storm. The world feels threatening.

Mood shift · Discovery

Discovery

A hidden map, ruins in the green, a secret revealed. Curiosity takes over.

Mood shift · Teamwork

Teamwork

Four children working together to survive. Hope, grit, belonging.

Key elements of the story to draw on

The Amazon setting. A vast, beautiful, dangerous rainforest.
Survival. Finding food, water, shelter and a way out.
Four children's journey. Strangers who become a team.
The mystery of the explorer. Who lived here before them, and why?

Try symbolic imagery, not literal scenes

Suggest the story rather than acting it out. A single object can carry a whole feeling.

Dense rainforest A broken compass Tangled vines An old map Firelight A small plane
06 Make it · digital tips

Filming and editing tips

Keep it simple. A clear voice, steady images and good pacing beat fancy effects every time.

Finding images

Make your own photos or drawings, or use copyright-free images.
Good free sources: photosforclass and Unsplash.

Filming

Film with steady shots and natural lighting.
Record a clear voiceover with short, suspenseful lines, for example: "Four children. One crashed plane. A jungle full of secrets."

Editing

Use iMovie or Canva. Trim clips for pace and match timing to your mood shifts.
Add text overlays, use transitions sparingly, and keep it moving.
Use only one or two quotes from the novel. Less is more.
Edit for spelling, audio clarity, flow and pacing before you finish.

Finishing & sharing

Add credits, for example: "Images from Unsplash. Music from Mixkit."
Export as an MP4 for viewing.
Share via Google Drive or Google Sites. Ask Mr Ell if you need a hand with Sites.
